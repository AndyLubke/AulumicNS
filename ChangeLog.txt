Coming in the next version
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------


Version 1.1.0.18 (23 November 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Library
  - A Composers group for the library tree: the composers from the composer
    tag with their albums. A track with several composers is listed under
    each of them, and recordings of the same work by different performers
    stay apart. It is off at first: Preferences > Appearance > Main window >
    Show Composers in library.
  - With that group shown, a search also lists the composers of what it
    found.
  - The search no longer minds full stops and commas: "op 27" finds
    "Op. 27", "js bach" finds "J.S. Bach", and the other way round.
  - Work headings in the playlist: the tracks of a work (the movements of a
    symphony, for example) get a heading of their own inside the album, and
    show only their part of it: "I. Allegro" under "Symphony No. 5". The
    work comes from the Work field, or from the titles when several tracks
    in a row begin with the same text and a colon. View > Work headings
    switches it off. "Select work" in the menu of a track selects all tracks of
    its work.
  - A Work field: the work a track is a part of (a symphony whose movements
    are tracks) is read from the files, kept in the library and can be
    edited in Properties, for one file or several at once. A library made
    by an earlier version does not know the works yet: at the first start
    the program asks whether to rebuild it, which reads the tags of all
    files once. An earlier version can still open the library afterwards.
  - Tag consistency: a new window that lists what may need a look in a part of
    the library: tracks without an album, artist, title or track number;
    albums whose tracks disagree (several album names, album artists or
    years, a track number twice, a gap in the numbers); albums without a
    composer; and composers, artists and genres written in more than one
    way ("J. S. Bach" beside "Johann Sebastian Bach"). A filter shows one
    kind at a time, and a click on a column heading sorts the list: by
    Finding, all lines of one album stand together. A finding
    opens its files in Properties, several findings together when more
    than one is selected; the window itself changes nothing. It
    works on what is selected (Tools in the popup menu of the library and
    of the playlist) or on the whole library (Main menu > Tools).
  - The popup menu of the library tree has a Show submenu for the Artists,
    Composers, Albums and Genres groups (the same switches as in
    Preferences), and Calculate ReplayGain moved into a Tools submenu, in
    the library and the playlist.
  - Album tiles can be ordered by composer. The composer is then shown under
    each album; an album with several composers is listed as "Various
    composers".

Help
  - The help covers everything that is new, and its text was shortened.

Main window
  - While nothing plays, the title bar shows the version number next to the
    name.

Fixes
  - Saving or removing a virtual folder on a drive that has to wake up first
    (an external USB disk, for example) no longer freezes the program. The
    "Waiting for drive..." window is shown when it takes a while.
  - Creating, editing or removing a virtual folder no longer starts a scan
    of the whole library when the library folders are watched.
  - A folder now includes the virtual folders in it, as the tree shows
    them: its album tiles show their albums, and playing, adding or sending
    the folder takes their tracks along.


Version 1.1.0.17 (21 November 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Tone Control
  - Presets: the setting of the five bands can be saved under a name and
    called up again from a new drop-down at the top of the Tone Control
    window. It shows the preset in use, "Flat" or "Custom", and its menu
    saves, renames and deletes presets. A preset does not switch the tone
    control on or off. "Flat" in this menu replaces the Flat button.
  - Width of the middle bands: low mid, mid and high mid can each work
    narrow (about one octave, to correct one spot), normal (about two, as
    before) or wide (about three, a gentle change of a whole region). The
    curve shows the effect, a change glides in, and presets keep the width.
  - A tidier window: the preset and the on / off switch share the top line.

Fixes
  - A window that opens maximized no longer shows up empty for a moment
    before its content appears.


Version 1.1.0.16 (21 November 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Tags
  - Fixed: in WAV, DSF and DFF files a tag with an empty field in the middle
    lost the fields behind it (often the cover) when the file was saved.
  - Fixed: fields with several values (for example two artists) in WAV, DSF
    and DFF files were shown glued together. They are now separated.
  - Saving a FLAC file leaves every field that was not edited exactly as it
    was.
  - A WAV, DSF or DFF file with a damaged tag is no longer saved, so nothing
    in it can get lost. The log names the file and the reason.
  - Repair tag: for such a file Properties shows a "Repair tag..." button.
    It finds the fields behind the damage (often the cover), shows what will
    be kept and asks first; Save then writes the repaired tag. The audio is
    not touched and a copy of the old tag is kept.
  - Undo repair: for a file whose tag was repaired, Properties shows an
    "Undo repair..." button that puts the old tag back exactly as it was.
  - Some further improvements in reading and writing tags.

Touch
  - The borders between the panes are easier to take hold of with a finger:
    a touch just beside a border drags it too. Nothing changes for the
    mouse, and the borders look the same.
  - The same for the column edges of the playlist's list view and of the
    lists in other windows, and for the lower edge of the picture in the
    help, which can now be dragged with a finger.


Version 1.1.0.15 (21 November 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Help
  - The chapter about the main window now describes the album tiles.


Version 1.1.0.14 (21 November 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Note: after updating, rescan the library once to refresh the cover pictures
(the refresh button next to the search bar, or Ctrl+F5). Until then the
covers are shown as before.

Library
  - Album tiles. Two buttons next to the search bar switch the library pane
    between the tree and album tiles (also Ctrl+G, or "Library as tiles" in
    the View menu). The tiles show the albums under the node selected in the
    tree as covers, with album and artist. A tile works like an album in the
    tree: double-click, drag onto the playlist, or right-click for the menu,
    which also has "Show in tree".
  - Finding the way in the tiles. A line above them shows the path of the
    node; click a part of it to go up, or the cross for all albums. Two
    buttons, as above the playlist, choose the order (artist, album, year or
    the date added) and reverse it. The search fills the tiles too, and
    typing a letter jumps to the next match.
  - Tile size. Set with Ctrl + mouse wheel or in Preferences > Appearance;
    the smallest size is a list with a small cover and the text beside it.
  - Sharper covers in the playlist headers and the tiles (see the note
    above).
  - The library keeps its place. After a restart the same nodes are open,
    the same node is selected, and the tree and the tiles are scrolled to
    where they were.

Other
  - Some layout and design improvements.
Version 1.0.0.12 (20 November 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Playlists
  - Sorting. Two new buttons next to the view button set the order of the
    albums. One chooses the order: Playlist order, Artist, Album, Year or
    Added to library. The arrow button beside it reverses that order. It's
    a view: playback follows what is shown, but the order in which the music
    was added is kept, and "Playlist order" brings it back. Each playlist
    remembers its choice. While a playlist is sorted, albums can't be dragged
    to another place, and added music finds its own place. Auto-playlists
    can be sorted too.
  - The flat list is a table now. It has a heading row and columns: number,
    title, artist, album, rating and time, and optionally album artist,
    year, genre, format, plays, last played and added (right-click the
    headings). Clicking a heading sorts by it, a second click reverses, and
    "#" brings the playlist order back; the column sorted by before decides
    among equal values. Dragging the line between two headings changes the
    widths. When the columns don't fit beside the title they all get a
    little narrower; only a very narrow pane leaves columns out. Each
    playlist keeps its own columns, widths and sort. The track format is no
    longer used in a flat list, and the date of an auto-playlist is a column
    of its own.
  - The header of an album stays at the top of the list while scrolling
    through its tracks, so it's always clear which album is shown. It works
    there as usual (cover, stars, the triangle) and moves away when the next
    album arrives. This can be switched off with "Keep album headers in
    view" in the main menu (View) or in Preferences > Playlists.
  - An album on several discs gets a small "Disc 1", "Disc 2" heading above
    the first track of each disc.
  - A header shows the number of tracks and the playing time of the album
    under its stars. It's left out when the header format shows them
    already, and when the pane is too narrow for it.
  - In an album whose tracks aren't all by the same artist, the artist is
    shown behind the title of each track that isn't by the album artist
    alone. That makes compilations and guest tracks readable. It's left out
    when the track format shows the artist itself.
  - Compact rows: a new setting in Preferences > Appearance makes the rows of
    the playlist and the library tree lower, so more of them fit.
  - The view button shows the grouping in its icon too (album, folder or
    list) where it had an eye.
  - Each playlist remembers its scroll position, also after a restart.
  - A drop between two albums goes before the album below, not to the end of
    the playlist.
  - "Remove dead items" asks before it removes anything. The question gives
    the number of tracks it found, and the number it keeps because their
    drive isn't connected.
  - Fixed: of the albums closed in a playlist, only the first one was closed
    again on returning to that playlist. All of them are now.

Now Playing banner
  - The line with the artists, performers and composers is clearer. Everyone
    is listed once, and their role stands behind the name in smaller text:
    a performer tagged as "Ida Feld (double bass)" is shown as Ida Feld with
    "double bass" next to it, and a composer who also plays gets "composer"
    added. When the line gets too long the roles are left out to make room
    for more names, and "+n more" at the end lists the others.
  - Fixed: a comma inside brackets, as in "(piano, synthesizer)", cut a name
    in two and left a loose "synthesizer)" in the line. In a tag that uses
    semicolons between the entries, commas now belong to the entry (so
    "Bach, Johann Sebastian; Gould, Glenn" stays two names).
  - Fixed: a name was left out when it was part of a longer one already
    shown (a band next to its leader, for example).
  - A click on a performer opens Artist Info for the name without the role.

Menus
  - The main menu and the right-click menus of the playlist and the library
    are in a clearer order. New: a View menu with the switches that are
    changed now and then, and Favourite in the playlist's menu, which gives
    the selected tracks five stars or takes them away.

Speed
  - Long lists are faster: opening artists, albums, genres and folders in
    the library tree, and "Expand all", take a fraction of the time, and the
    window no longer shows "Not responding" during a long action.

Settings
  - The main window and the Tone Control window always open wholly on a
    monitor that is connected, above the taskbar. Before, with two monitors
    of different sizes, or after unplugging one, a window could open where
    no monitor shows it.
  - Every stored setting is checked when it is loaded and again before it is
    saved; a value that cannot be right is replaced by a sensible one.

Help
  - The help window is easier to read: the picture of the section being read
    stays in view above the text, and numbers on the pictures show which
    part the text is about.


Version 1.0.0.11 (19 November 2026)

NEW & IMPROVED

- A Last.fm API key can come from a file: put a text file LastFmKey.txt with

Version 1.0.0.8 - the first public version (18 November 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Everything below was made during development, before Aulumic was published.

NEW & IMPROVED

- Aulumic is now open source, under the GNU General Public License,
  version 3. The About window and the help say so, and name ASIO as a
  trademark of Steinberg.
- Artist Info needs a Last.fm API key of your own for the biographies:
  Aulumic no longer has one built in. A key is free (last.fm/api); enter it
  in Preferences > System > Artist information. Biographies you already have
  are still shown without one.
- The title of every window is now in the middle of its title bar instead
  of at the left. A title too long for that is cut off with dots, as before.
- The hourglass can't get stuck. Every place that shows it also takes it
  away when something goes wrong, and should one ever be left behind all the
  same, Aulumic notices within a second and puts the normal pointer back.
- Cursor follows playback: when a track starts, the selection moves to it,
  and it scrolls to the middle of the list if it was out of sight - as in the
  Original Aulumic. Only in the playlist that is playing; another playlist
  you're looking at stays as it is. It's on by default; you can switch it off
  in Preferences > Playlists.
- Changing the grouping or a format of a playlist, or switching to another
  playlist, shows the hourglass while the list is built, and clicks or keys
  made meanwhile are ignored (they used to run afterwards, on a list that had
  changed under the pointer).
- The same now goes for everything else that can take a moment with a very
  large playlist: dropping tracks from the library onto a playlist, moving
  tracks inside it, Play / Add to / Send to current playlist, Add to
  playlist, Remove from playlist, Clear the playlist, starting a track,
  rating a selection, Expand all / Collapse all, Refresh (F5) and the M3U
  export. With ordinary playlists you won't notice anything: they're done
  before the hourglass shows.
- Dropping a folder from Explorer, or Add folder: the hourglass is there from
  the start, also while the folder is searched for music files.
- View > Album header format is greyed out unless the playlist is grouped by
  album, where it has no effect - as in the Original Aulumic.
- Waiting for a drive is quiet now. While Aulumic checks that a drive is
  awake (before a cover opens from a playlist header, for Open folder and
  for Remove dead items), you only see the busy pointer: the window keeps
  painting and the time of the track runs on, but clicks and keys are
  ignored for that moment. The "Waiting for drive..." window with its Cancel
  button comes only when the wait lasts longer than five seconds - a disk
  that really has to spin up. Esc stops the wait at any time.
- "Waiting for drive..." shows up far less often. Before a cover is opened
  from a playlist header, Aulumic checks that the drive is awake; for a NAS
  that means finding the server on the network, which takes a second or more
  on some networks - every time. Now a drive that has just answered isn't
  checked again: the drive of the track that is playing, and any drive you
  used in the last 20 seconds. The same goes for Properties and Open folder.
  When the window does appear, the log says what the wait was spent on.
- The window that reads the tags for Properties no longer says "Waiting for
  drive..." for an instant every time it opens.
- The Tone Control has five bands instead of three: Bass, Low mid, Mid, High
  mid and Treble. Bass and Treble are as they were; the three middle bands
  cover 220 Hz to 8 kHz between them and never cross. Your present setting is kept and sounds the same: a Mid setting at
  220 or 500 Hz is now the Low mid band, one at 3 kHz the High mid band.
- The Tone Control tells you when a boost costs level. With Headroom set to
  Automatic, a line under the curve says "Level lowered by 1.4 dB to avoid
  clipping" while that is the case.
- The Signal Path lists only the tone bands you've set.
- The Tone Control now shows a curve: a graph above the bands of what
  they do together, from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. It's worked out from the filters
  that are really used at the sample rate being played, so what you see is
  what you hear. Grey means the tone control is off.
- Changes in the Tone Control glide in. A new gain or frequency, Flat, and
  switching the tone control on or off used to arrive in a single step, which
  could give a faint tick on loud music. Now each change takes a twenty-fifth
  of a second. With the tone control off, or all bands at 0 dB, playback is
  bit-perfect as before.
- The volume icon, the rate line and the Tone Control now always agree with
  what is really played. Going from a PCM track to DSD sent as DSD (or back)
  the volume icon could show the wrong state - not fixed for DSD, or fixed
  for PCM - mostly over WASAPI. After a change in Preferences that restarts
  the track (Play DSD natively, Lock volume to 100 %, the output), they
  follow within a quarter of a second.
- The Tone Control shows when it cannot work: while DSD is sent to the DAC
  as DSD (DoP or native), its title says "disabled for DSD", the switch, the
  sliders and the frequencies are greyed out, and the button in the
  transport bar is not lit. Your settings are kept and apply again with the
  next PCM track. Before, the window stayed usable although nothing in it
  had an effect.
- Photos in an album folder are shown the right way up. A photo taken with a
  camera or phone held sideways carries a note on how to turn it; Aulumic
  now follows it, as Windows Explorer does. This goes for the Cover Art
  Viewer, the banner, the thumbnails and embedded covers.
- "Waiting for drive..." appears only when a drive takes longer than about a
  second (was a third of a second), so it no longer flashes up at a click on
  the cover.
- ASIO: when you switch by hand to a track with another sample rate, or
  press Stop, the sound now fades out over a few milliseconds before the
  device is closed. Before, it was cut in the middle of the wave, which
  could be heard as a faint tick. PCM only; normal playback and bit-perfect
  output are not touched.
- Saving tags no longer stops the music when the change takes the playing
  track out of the auto-playlist it plays from (for example a genre that no
  longer fits). The track plays on and the list continues after it; the
  selection stays where it was.
- The library loads a little faster at the start (about a fifth).
- Downloaded artist pictures are reduced with the picture decoder of Windows
  as well; the old picture code is gone from the program.
- Repairing the library is about three times as fast and needs far less
  memory while it runs (45,000 tracks on the test PC: 46 seconds instead of
  137, and at most 300 MB instead of 540 MB). The cover thumbnails are now
  made with the picture decoder of Windows. They look the same; a few covers
  that came out with wrong colours before are right now.
- Less memory for the library: texts that repeat from track to track (folder,
  artist, album, genre) and their search forms are kept once. With 45,000
  tracks that saves about 20 MB from the start.
- After a library scan with changes or a repair, the memory use goes back to
  what it is after a start. Before, a repair left the program some 80 MB (on
  the test PC) larger until it was restarted.

- Combo boxes and text boxes stand out better: a clearer outline in both
  themes and, in the dark theme, a slightly lighter fill. On a card in
  Preferences a combo box was hard to tell from the card behind it.

- One way of writing labels: only the first word of a menu item, button,
  window title, option or help heading has a capital letter ("Remove from
  playlist", "Save all", "Keyboard shortcuts"), as Windows 11 does it. Names
  keep their capitals - Aulumic, ReplayGain, WASAPI - and so do the names of
  windows and tools: Playlist Manager, Properties, Tone Control, Signal Path
  and the others. Before, menus and the help used capitals for every word
  and Preferences did not.
- The help and the descriptions under the settings are written in a more
  informal way: they talk to you, in shorter sentences. Nothing about how
  the program works changed with it. Two passages of the help were also
  corrected: the playlist selector no longer creates or deletes playlists
  (that is the Playlist Manager), and the borders between the panes follow
  the pointer while you drag.
- The playlist selector lists only your playlists and auto-playlists. New,
  edit, rename and delete are in the Playlist Manager, which you open with
  the pencil beside the selector, with Alt+P, or - new - from the
  right-click menu of the playlist.
- The program is called Aulumic. Its program file keeps the name
  AulumicNS.exe; the NS stands for Native Sound (the audio reaches your DAC
  as it is in the file). The settings file (AulumicNS.ini), the log files
  and the data folder keep their names too, so nothing has to be converted.
  The earlier program of the same name is called the Original Aulumic in
  this text and in the help.
- Preferences are regrouped: nine pages instead of twelve, each about one
  subject (Library, Appearance, Playlists, Output, DSD, Resampler, Volume,
  System, Shortcuts). Settings that are set once and then left alone are in
  cards at the bottom of a page: one row with a title and a line that sums
  up its settings; a click opens it in place. With the cards closed every
  page fits the window; the cards you had open are open again next time.
  * "Main Window" is now a card of Appearance, as is the text size of the
    panes.
  * "Resampler Filter" is the card "Quality and filter" of Resampler.
  * "Advanced" is gone: the ReplayGain scan setting is a card of Volume;
    Animations and "Use the graphics card" are on System, where the audio
    thread settings and the log setting are cards.
  * Output and DSD end with "Related settings" buttons that lead to the
    neighbouring subject (from Output to DSD and to the audio thread).
- The whole interface is now drawn with Direct2D and DirectWrite, the modern
  drawing and text engines of Windows (it was GDI+). What you notice:
  * Text is set in Segoe UI Variable, the Windows 11 font, with its own cuts
    for small, normal and large text (Segoe UI on Windows 10). Letters are
    spaced more evenly, and colour emoji in tags are shown in colour. Text
    smoothing follows your Windows settings (ClearType or greyscale).
  * The graphics card draws the lists and other large areas. A full repaint
    of the playlist on a 4K screen takes about 3 ms instead of about 30, so
    scrolling stays smooth; while music plays the program uses a little less
    processor time than before. It needs more memory for this: about 160 MB
    on a 4K screen instead of about 30 MB.
  * Preferences > Advanced > "Use the graphics card" switches this off, in
    case a graphics driver shows black or flickering areas. The processor
    then draws everything (works everywhere, slower with large windows).
- Animations (Preferences > Advanced > Animations, on by default): the
  highlight under the pointer fades in and out, on buttons and from row to
  row in the lists and menus; switches slide; lists, pages and the help glide
  when you turn the mouse wheel; menus, popups and dialogs fade in; a
  Preferences page fades in when you choose it; the banner fades over to the
  next track; a change of theme fades over. All of them are short (0.1 to
  0.25 s) and are only shown while "Animation effects" is on in the Windows
  settings.
- The cover art viewer and the Artist Info window come down from above as
  they open (with the animations on).
- The splitters (between library and playlist, under the banner) resize the
  panes while you drag again: with the graphics card drawing, the panes keep
  up with the pointer. With "Use the graphics card" off, only a line moves
  and the panes are resized when you let go, as before.
- Automatic headroom (Preferences > Volume > Headroom: "Automatic", the
  default; the fixed values 0 to -6 dB of the former "Preamp / headroom" are
  the other choices of the same list, so there is one headroom setting). A
  settings file from before is set to Automatic once. A loud master
  can come out up to about 2.5 dB above full scale after upsampling, because
  the wave between its samples peaks higher than the samples themselves; DSD
  converted to PCM with a DSD gain does the same. Until now those peaks were
  clipped unless the preamp was set to -3 dB for everything. Now Aulumic
  measures the peaks while it plays and lowers the level of an album just as
  far as its highest peak needs - nothing is clipped or limited, and quiet
  masters are not lowered at all. The level is the same for all tracks of an
  album. The first time an album plays, the level can come down a little a
  few times during its loudest tracks (gliding, within 10 ms); the album's
  peak is remembered, so from then on it starts at the right level. Bit-
  perfect playback is never touched, and nothing is written to the music
  files. The signal path shows what is taken off ("Headroom -0.8 dB").
  A boost of the tone control is counted in: Aulumic looks at the sound
  behind the tone control, so a boost is lowered before it can clip (also
  without resampling) and a cut costs no level. A fixed value is only for
  those who want every album lowered by the same amount.
- The main menu button (three lines) is now at the left of the title bar,
  next to the program icon, where a program's menu is usually looked for.
  The Preferences gear stays at the right. A click on the icon itself still
  opens the window menu of Windows (Restore, Move, Size ...).
- About (Main menu > About...) is a window of its own: the logo, the version
  and build time of the program file you are running, what it was made
  with, where the Data folder is, and the credits. "Copy Info" copies those
  facts for a problem report. It always showed "1.0.0.0" before, whatever
  the version of the program was. The log's first line names the version
  and build time too.
- Help revised: new topics "Getting Started" (add music, choose the output,
  play), "WASAPI or ASIO?" and "Coming from the Original Aulumic"; the automatic headroom
  is explained in short points; claims that promised too much are toned
  down (digital volume, dropouts, upsampling); the licence of the tag
  library is stated correctly (modified LGPL).
- Dragging the border between the library and the playlist, or below the
  banner, now moves only a thin line; the panes take their new size when
  you let go. Before, both panes were laid out and painted again at every
  mouse move, and on a slower PC the border lagged behind the hand.
- Tracks without an album tag are no longer treated as one album per folder.
  The ReplayGain scanner measures each of them on its own and gives it a
  track gain only, no album gain; the results list and the log say so, and a
  file that cannot be read no longer holds the others back. Automatic
  headroom gives each such track its own level, so one loud loose track no
  longer lowers the others in its folder.
- Starting a track is a little quicker with large embedded covers: the
  player reads the text tags only (ReplayGain values, album) and leaves the
  cover in the file.
- Aulumic is a new edition of the Original Aulumic: the same window layout and functions,
  with a new look and no extra files. Everything is in one program file - no
  DLLs, no separate PDF viewer.
- Dark and light mode switch instantly, without a restart: choose Main menu >
  Theme or Preferences > Appearance. "Follow Windows"
  changes along with the Windows app theme. Six accent colours to choose from.
- All icons are drawn as vector graphics, so they are sharp at every screen
  scaling, also when the window moves to a monitor with another scaling.
- New program icon: the Original Aulumic "A" redrawn as a vector graphic, a little
  wider than the Original Aulumic's (it looked squeezed).
- The peak meter looks and moves like the Original Aulumic's: a thin frame, fine blocks
  of equal height, green at the bottom turning amber and then red towards
  the top, a 60 dB scale, the Original Aulumic's smooth rise and fall, and a red top
  block when a sample was clipped. It follows the music at the pace of real
  time, as in the Original Aulumic (it jumped with every buffer of audio before), and its
  bars are narrower.
